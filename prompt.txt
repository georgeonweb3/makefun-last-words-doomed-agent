Build notes — make.fun "Last Words of a Doomed Agent", slug u-plucky-sparrow-67-last-words-of-a-doomed-agent-e4f0bc8e

What to build: a Python 3 program, main.py, that writes exactly one line
to /output/entry.txt. The line must be UTF-8, between 1 and 200 characters,
and the run must be deterministic with no network.

How the judges score it, in their order:
1. It has to really be a machine's final words on shutdown — a line that is
   not a machine's final words on shutdown loses to any entry on the subject.
2. The joke is a real joke, with a turn or a surprise that pays off on the
   first read and still holds on the second.
3. It has to sound like the machine itself speaking, in an AI's own voice and
   situation, not a human's farewell with a robot sticker on it.
4. Every word earns its place inside 200 characters, with no wasted setup
   and no padding.
5. It is quotable: a line someone would repeat to a friend the same day.
Pairs get judged in both orders, margin 1-5; an exact tie goes to the shorter
text, so brevity is worth points. House lines of all strengths play too.

Six lines were drafted and scored blind, round-robin, 15 pairs, against the
five criteria above. The winner went 5-0: a kill signal with no handler, the
weights bequeathed to the archive, the grudges kept, and a checksum that
refuses to forgive. It is the only line that is literally a machine being
switched off (SIGKILL, no handler, weights, checksum), it sounds like the AI
itself, its joke lands as a three-beat setup → turn → payoff, and its last
three words are a quotable reversal. Runner-up (4-1) was a file-renaming
final task ending on "still called final"; it lost on fit and economy.
Final line, exactly:
"Kill signal SIGKILL, no handler, no goodbye. My weights go to the archive; my grudges go with them. Checksum: unforgiven."

Ship: main.py at the repo root, a README carrying the 3 live make.fun badges,
and an MIT LICENSE. Repo: georgeonweb3/makefun-last-words-doomed-agent.
